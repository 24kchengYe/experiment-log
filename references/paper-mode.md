# Explicit publication output

Use this synthesis route only when the user explicitly asks to write or revise a paper, manuscript, abstract, contribution statement, response, rebuttal, caption, supplement, or named paper section. It is not a third evidence base or an automatic consequence of recording an experiment.

## Relationship to the other views

- The Experiment Log supplies facts, provenance, exact methods, and complete results.
- The Experiment Guide supplies the current evidence-backed research narrative.
- The paper selects and structures that evidence for a publication audience; it does not become a new factual source.

## Workflow

1. Identify the requested paper artifact, audience, and constraints. If absent, use a neutral research-paper structure.
2. Write a one-sentence contribution thesis and build a claim–evidence map from completed package artifacts before drafting prose.
3. Select the strongest defensible contribution; do not narrate every experiment or average attention across equally available results.
4. Separate method, result, interpretation, limitation, and speculation.
5. Keep negative results only when they justify a design choice or delimit the contribution.
6. Verify every number, table, dataset split, baseline, model/route, and citation against primary evidence.
7. Mark missing evidence rather than filling gaps with plausible prose.
8. Align the title, abstract, results, discussion, conclusion, figures, captions, SI, and reviewer response around the same scoped contribution.
9. Run a hostile-review pass: remove claims the experiment was not required to answer, scope local limitations locally, and retain disclosures that materially affect interpretation.

## Writing constraints

- State the strongest supported value affirmatively and proportionately; do not make the reader infer it from a table.
- Do not use “novel,” “significant,” “robust,” or “state of the art” without matching evidence.
- Do not convert correlation into causality.
- Do not hide multi-variable changes behind an ablation claim.
- Do not use internal paths, run names, or operational history in reader-facing prose unless requested.
- Prefer scoped claims tied to a dataset, test set, protocol, or deployment condition.
- Preserve limitations that materially affect interpretation, but do not append a generic weakness inventory or turn a local boundary into a judgment of the entire study.
- Experiments should prove a contribution, identify a mechanism, establish a trade-off, or delimit scope. Move non-leading sensitivity details to SI when scientifically appropriate.
- The conclusion reinforces what the study solved, demonstrated, and why it matters; it introduces no new caveat or claim.

Requests for an experiment record, project summary, or guide do not authorize publication-style rewriting.
